PLC TROUBLESHOOTING AND MAINTENANCE GUIDE
Document ID: PLC-MNT-003
Revision: 1.0

1. PLC Communication Failure
Common symptoms of a communication problem include loss of data exchange, timeout alarms, stale status values, or a device showing an offline state. First verify power and physical network connections, then check the PLC and network diagnostics available from the control system.

2. Power Checks
Before inspecting electrical hardware, follow the site's electrical safety procedure. Verify that the PLC power supply and relevant field-device power supplies are within their specified operating conditions.

3. I/O Diagnosis
Compare the expected I/O state with the observed state in the PLC diagnostic or monitoring interface. A mismatch can result from field wiring, sensor/actuator issues, logic conditions, or communication problems.

4. Fault Reset
A reset should not be treated as the first troubleshooting step for a recurring fault. Capture the alarm or diagnostic information, investigate the cause, and reset only according to the approved procedure.

5. Backup
Keep an approved backup of PLC programs and configuration data according to site policy. Do not overwrite a working controller program with an unverified backup.

6. Escalation
If a fault remains unresolved after the approved checks, escalate it to qualified controls or electrical personnel. Do not bypass interlocks or safety logic to force machine operation.
